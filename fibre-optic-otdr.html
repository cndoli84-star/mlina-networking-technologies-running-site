FIBRE INFRASTRUCTURE

Fibre Optic Cable Installation

Fibre optic cabling provides a high-capacity communications backbone for buildings, campuses, industrial facilities and links between network locations. We support installation planning and deployment based on the route, environment, required capacity and project specification.

Scope may include route preparation, cable pulling or blowing where appropriate, duct and containment coordination, cable protection, labelling and installation checks. The final method is selected for the site conditions and cable manufacturer's requirements.

JOINTING & TERMINATION

Fibre Splicing and Termination

Fibre links require controlled jointing and correctly prepared terminations to support dependable optical performance. Services can include fusion splicing, splice tray organisation, joint closure installation, pigtail termination, fibre distribution panels and optical patching.

We plan fibre core allocation and labelling around the agreed design, helping make the installed infrastructure easier to identify, test, maintain and expand.

TESTING & ACCEPTANCE

OTDR Testing and Fibre Link Verification

Optical Time-Domain Reflectometer (OTDR) testing is used to examine fibre links, locate reflective and non-reflective events, assess attenuation along the link and identify potential faults such as breaks, poor splices or connector-related events.

Testing scope is agreed against the project specification and may include suitable test wavelengths, launch and receive arrangements, trace review, event analysis and documented results. OTDR testing can be complemented by optical loss testing where required by the acceptance plan.

FAULT FINDING

Fibre Fault Location and Troubleshooting

For existing links, fault investigation can help narrow down the location and nature of optical events affecting connectivity. Assessment may include reviewing link records, inspecting accessible patching and termination points, cleaning and checking connectors, and carrying out appropriate optical tests.

Findings can be documented to support repair planning and decisions on whether a splice, connector, patch lead or cable section requires attention.

DOCUMENTATION

Fibre Test Reports and Handover Records

Clear records help network owners understand what was installed and how the links performed at the time of testing. Depending on the agreed deliverables, handover documentation may include fibre core schedules, route and termination labels, OTDR traces, test summaries, identified events and outstanding observations.

Test limits and acceptance criteria should be defined in the project specification before testing begins. Reports describe the measurements taken; they do not imply third-party accreditation unless separately established.

RELATED INFRASTRUCTURE

Fibre Optics for LAN, WAN and Campus Networks

Fibre infrastructure can interconnect buildings, network cabinets, server rooms, distribution points and remote facilities. We can coordinate fibre works with structured copper cabling, racks, patch panels, switches and wider LAN/WAN deployment requirements.

For a complete solution, discuss the network topology, link distances, bandwidth needs, redundancy objectives and active equipment interfaces with our team.

Our Fibre Project Delivery Process

1. Requirements and site review

Review the route, site conditions, link endpoints, fibre type, core count, pathway availability and project deliverables.

2. Installation and termination

Coordinate cable deployment, protection, splicing, termination, patching and labelling in line with the approved scope.

3. Testing and handover

Carry out the agreed optical tests, review results against specified limits and prepare the agreed test records and handover information.

Information to Include in a Fibre Optic Enquiry

To help us assess your requirements and prepare a relevant quotation, share as much of the following as is available:

  • Project site location and number of buildings or network locations.
  • Approximate fibre route length and whether the route is indoor, outdoor, underground or aerial.
  • Fibre type and core count, if already specified.
  • Number of links, termination points, splice closures and fibre distribution panels.
  • Whether the requirement is new installation, repair, testing of existing links or a combination.
  • Required test scope, reporting format, drawings, BOQ and project timeline.
