LOAD ASSESSMENT

UPS Sizing and Power Requirements

UPS selection starts with the equipment load, not just a headline VA rating. We assess connected watts and VA, power factor, peak or starting demand, voltage, phase, criticality and the runtime required for an orderly shutdown or continued operation.

A load schedule and site information help determine capacity, expansion allowance and the appropriate UPS configuration. Final selection must be checked against manufacturer specifications and the actual installation conditions.

UPS TOPOLOGY

Online, Line-Interactive and Standby UPS

Different applications may call for online double-conversion, line-interactive or standby UPS technology. The appropriate topology depends on the connected equipment, input power quality, transfer requirements, sensitivity, operating environment and budget.

We help assess rack-mounted and tower configurations for network cabinets, server rooms, communications equipment and other business-critical ICT loads.

BACKUP DURATION

Battery Banks and Runtime Planning

Backup duration is influenced by the real load, battery capacity and condition, battery configuration, UPS efficiency, temperature and operating conditions. Runtime should be calculated from suitable manufacturer data and verified for the proposed system.

Where extended autonomy is required, battery capacity, charging capability, space, ventilation and maintenance requirements should be considered together.

BATTERY SERVICE

UPS Battery Inspection and Replacement

Ageing or failed batteries can reduce runtime and compromise power continuity. Battery service may include condition checks, replacement planning and installation of compatible battery sets for the specified UPS model.

Battery voltage, chemistry, capacity, connector arrangement and manufacturer requirements must be confirmed before replacement. Safe isolation and handling procedures are essential.

ICT & ELV PROTECTION

Power Backup for Network and Security Systems

UPS protection can be planned for servers, switches, routers, wireless controllers, fibre equipment, IP PBX systems, CCTV recorders, access control equipment and essential workstation loads.

Critical and non-critical equipment should be separated so that available capacity is prioritised. Network and security loads may also need PoE budget and startup demand to be included in calculations.

POWER QUALITY

Bypass, Surge Protection and Earthing Coordination

UPS installations may require maintenance bypass arrangements, suitable upstream and downstream circuit protection, correctly specified surge protective devices and coordination with the site's earthing system.

A UPS does not replace proper earthing, circuit protection or a compliant electrical installation. Upstream electrical works should be undertaken by appropriately qualified electrical personnel and clearly defined in the project scope.

SITE INTEGRATION

Generator Compatibility and Monitoring

Where a generator is part of the backup arrangement, compatibility should be checked against generator voltage and frequency regulation, UPS input characteristics, load profile and manufacturer guidance.

Supported UPS models may provide status monitoring, event logs, alarms or network management functions. Monitoring options depend on the UPS, communications card, software and network environment.

LIFECYCLE SUPPORT

UPS Commissioning, Maintenance and Fault Diagnosis

Commissioning may include inspection, configuration, operational checks, alarm review, transfer and bypass checks where applicable, battery status review and handover of operating information.

Preventive maintenance and troubleshooting can be arranged according to equipment condition, manufacturer recommendations, access requirements and the agreed service level.

What to Include in a UPS Enquiry

Share the following information where available to help establish a suitable power backup design:

  • Site location and the type of environment: office, server room, network rack, school, CCTV or other facility.
  • Equipment list, quantity, nameplate ratings or measured watts/VA.
  • Required UPS output voltage and whether the supply/load is single-phase or three-phase.
  • Target backup runtime and whether the objective is ride-through, continued operation or controlled shutdown.
  • Critical and non-critical loads, including PoE switches and connected devices.
  • Existing UPS make, model, capacity, age and battery details, if applicable.
  • Available space, rack or tower preference, ventilation and access conditions.
  • Existing generator, bypass arrangement, surge protection and earthing information.
  • Preferred installation date and any preventive maintenance or support requirements.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you determine the right UPS capacity?

Capacity is assessed using connected load, peak demand, power factor, voltage and phase, runtime target, UPS topology and manufacturer specifications. A load list and site assessment are important.

How long will a UPS keep my equipment running?

Runtime depends on the actual load, battery capacity and condition, UPS efficiency and operating conditions. It should be calculated or verified against manufacturer data; the VA rating alone does not establish runtime.

Can you replace UPS batteries?

Battery replacement can be scoped after confirming the UPS model, battery type, voltage, capacity and manufacturer requirements.

Which UPS type is suitable for servers?

Online double-conversion is often considered for sensitive or critical loads, while line-interactive or standby designs may suit other applications. Selection depends on the equipment and site power conditions.

Can a UPS work with a generator?

Yes, where the generator output and UPS input requirements are compatible. Voltage/frequency regulation, load profile and manufacturer guidance should be checked.

Does a UPS replace proper earthing or surge protection?

No. Correct earthing, circuit protection and appropriately specified surge protection remain important parts of the electrical installation.

Do you provide UPS maintenance?

Maintenance may include inspection, alarm review, operational checks, battery condition review, ventilation checks and fault diagnosis, subject to the model and agreed scope.
