CONTROL & MANAGEMENT

Intruder Alarm Panels and Keypads

The alarm control panel manages detection zones, arming modes, user codes, system status and supported communication functions. Keypads, proximity tags or other compatible interfaces can be selected to suit how occupants use the premises.

Panel capacity, zone count, user permissions, partitioning and expansion options should be matched to the building layout and the intended security operation.

INTRUSION DETECTION

Motion and Occupancy Detectors

Passive infrared (PIR), dual-technology or other compatible motion detectors can be positioned to monitor internal spaces, circulation routes and designated protected areas.

Sensor selection and placement should account for room geometry, mounting height, temperature variation, airflow, direct sunlight, pets and normal site activity to help minimise unwanted activations.

PERIMETER & OPENINGS

Door, Window and Glass-Break Sensors

Magnetic contacts can monitor the opening of doors, windows, shutters and other access points. Glass-break detection may be considered for suitable glazed areas where the device and room conditions support reliable detection.

Zones can be arranged to distinguish entry points and protected areas, subject to panel capacity and the agreed operating plan.

ALERT & DETERRENCE

Sirens, Strobes and Panic Devices

Internal sounders, external sirens, visual indicators and panic or duress devices may be included where appropriate to the site's risk assessment and operational procedures.

Device locations, audible output, user access and any panic-alert workflow should be agreed during design. A panic button does not itself provide a staffed response service.

CONNECTIVITY

Remote Notifications and Alarm Communications

Some alarm platforms support event notifications through IP, cellular modules, applications or a separately contracted monitoring service. Available options depend on the panel, module, network coverage, software and provider.

Remote alerts should not be described as guaranteed delivery or staffed monitoring. Communication paths, subscription costs, backup connectivity and recipient responsibilities need to be confirmed.

RESILIENCE

Backup Batteries, Tamper Protection and Maintenance

Alarm systems may include standby batteries, enclosure and cable tamper supervision, power-failure reporting and periodic device checks where supported by the equipment.

Maintenance can include battery condition checks, sensor testing, panel event review, keypad and siren checks, communication tests and fault diagnosis. Backup duration depends on system load and battery condition.

What to Include in an Intruder Alarm Enquiry

Share these details to help us assess the requirement and prepare a practical proposal:

Frequently Asked Questions

What does an intruder alarm system include?

Depending on the site, it may include an alarm panel, keypad or tag reader, motion detectors, door/window contacts, glass-break sensors, panic devices, sounders, backup battery and supported communication modules.

Can you install wired and wireless intruder alarms?

Wired, wireless or hybrid systems may be suitable depending on the building, cable routes, radio conditions, device compatibility and project requirements. A site assessment helps determine the design.

Can an intruder alarm send alerts to a mobile phone?

Remote notifications may be available where the panel, communication module, network or cellular service and application support them. Connectivity and any third-party service requirements should be confirmed.

Will the alarm work during a power outage?

A suitable standby battery can support operation during a mains failure. Backup duration depends on battery capacity, system load, battery condition and configuration.

How can false alarms be reduced?

Correct sensor selection and positioning, suitable detection zones, correct installation, user training and maintenance help reduce false alarms. Pets and environmental conditions should be considered during design.

Do you provide 24-hour alarm monitoring or response?

Installing an alarm does not itself include staffed 24-hour monitoring or physical response. Any monitoring centre or response service must be separately confirmed and agreed.

Is an intruder alarm the same as a fire alarm?

No. Intruder alarms detect security events. Fire detection and life-safety alarm systems have different equipment and compliance requirements and should be specified separately.
